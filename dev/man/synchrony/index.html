Synchrony in space

How far apart agents can be and still be in step, measured two ways, each against a null.

The null: time-shift surrogates

Tierbrief

timeShift(p) rotates each track's phase series in time by its own random offset. Every agent keeps its rhythm; agents stop being aligned with each other.

Tierfull

The null asks: how in step would these agents look by chance, given their own rhythms? If the whole tissue is partly synchronised at some moment, even distant pairs score above 0, and a single agent's slow drift in frequency makes it look partly aligned with anything. Shifting each track as a whole keeps all of that and destroys only the alignment between agents.

Why not shuffle phases among agents within each frame? That keeps the mean right but makes the null band far too narrow. A fresh shuffle in each frame is independent of the shuffle in the next, so averaging over frames shrinks its spread as if every frame were an independent observation. The real phases are not: they are band-passed, so each agent's phase moves only about 2π·dt/period per frame and consecutive frames are correlated. The observed value therefore varies much more than the shuffled one, and p-values come out much too small.

PhaseCoherence and OrderParameter draw nsurrogates surrogates (19 by default, so the smallest p-value is 0.05) and report their mean as null, their 2.5–97.5% range as nulllow/nullhigh, and a one-sided pvalue. Raise nsurrogates for finer p-values; the cost grows linearly.

2026-10-15 — Time-shift surrogates as the null

Decided: the null for coherence and the order parameter is the time-shift surrogate, drawn per track. Rejected: the prototype's per-frame shuffle of phases among agents. Its mean is a fine baseline to subtract, but its shuffles are independent from frame to frame while the real phases are correlated across frames, so its band is far too narrow for p-values.

Coherence with distance

Tierbrief

PhaseCoherence averages cos(φᵢ − φⱼ) over pairs of agents in the same frame, binned by the distance between them.

Tierfull

cos(φᵢ − φⱼ) is 1 for two agents at the same point in their cycle, 0 a quarter cycle apart and −1 opposite. Bins are dr wide and centred on 0, dr, …, rmax. The first bin holds only the closest pairs; in PhysiCell these are compressed or newly divided cells and are real.

excess = coherence − null is the coherence due to proximity. dr and rmax are in the positions' units and have no defaults. frames selects which frames to pool. Because the phases are band-passed, consecutive frames are correlated when the band's shortest period spans many frames (with dt = 10 and band = (100, 300), a phase moves at most about 36° per frame), so frames = 1:2:n or sparser saves time for little loss. Compare two selections to check.

c = PhaseCoherence(p; dr = 10, rmax = 80, frames = 1:4:200, rng = Xoshiro(3))
PhaseCoherence: 9 distance bins to 80.0, 19 time-shift surrogates
  excess coherence: [0.89, 0.84, 0.69, 0.55, 0.4, 0.31, 0.28, 0.28, 0.27]

The local order parameter

Tierbrief

OrderParameter measures, around each agent, how aligned the phases within radius r are: R = |mean e^{iφ}|, averaged over agents and frames.

Tierfull

Each phase is a unit arrow; R is the length of the average arrow over the agents within r, the centre agent included. R = 1 means all point the same way. Random phases do not give 0: the average of n random unit arrows has length about 1/√n, so with few neighbours at small r, chance alone gives a large R. Read R against null. R below null means neighbours are less aligned than chance, as in lateral inhibition.

o = OrderParameter(p; radii = [10, 20, 40, 60], frames = 1:4:200, rng = Xoshiro(3))
OrderParameter: radii [10.0, 20.0, 40.0, 60.0], 19 time-shift surrogates
  R:    [0.95, 0.88, 0.72, 0.63]
  null: [0.49, 0.37, 0.21, 0.15]
Tierdev

Both readouts draw all surrogates up front (_phasesets) and accumulate observed and surrogate sums in the same pass over pairs, so the pair search runs once per frame. Memory is (1 + nsurrogates) copies of the phase arrays.

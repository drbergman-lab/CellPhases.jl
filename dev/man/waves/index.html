Phase waves

Around each agent, fit a local plane wave to the neighbours' phases: its gradient gives the direction of travel, and with the agent's own phase rate, the speed.

Direction

Tierbrief

PhaseWaves fits wrap(φⱼ − φᵢ) ≈ ∇φ · (xⱼ − xᵢ) by least squares over the neighbours within r (at least minneighbours), for every agent with a phase in the selected frames. The wave travels along −∇φ.

Tierfull

Wrapping maps each difference into (−π, π], so 350° against 10° counts as 20° apart. A phase point, say the peak, moves toward agents that have not reached it yet, which have lower φ: hence −∇φ. direction is that angle in radians, 0 along +x and π/2 along +y.

Each fit has an R². Where no single plane explains the neighbours (a wave front, a defect, or no wave at all) R² is low. Summaries use the fits with R² ≥ 0.5, and the share of such fits (cleanShare) must be reported with them: in the motivating model only 2–24% of cells had clean fits, depending on the signal.

w = PhaseWaves(p; r = 30, frames = 1:4:200)
good = w.r2 .>= 0.5
rad2deg(angle(mean(cis.(w.direction[good])))), cleanShare(w)
(90.03716504463719, 1.0)

Speed

Tierbrief

speed = ω / |∇φ|, with ω the agent's own phase advance to the next frame, in radians per time unit of dt. Speeds are in position units per that time unit, relative to the agents.

Tierfull

On a moving front the phase is constant. A point moving c·dt along the direction of travel sees its phase change by ω·dt − |∇φ|·c·dt = 0, so c = ω/|∇φ|. ∇φ comes from one frame; ω follows the same agent to the next frame by ID. Frames where the phase runs backward (ω ≤ 0) have no defined wave and get speed = NaN; the fit is kept for its direction.

Because ω follows the agent, the speed is relative to the tissue. The speed in the lab adds the agents' own motion along the wave.

median(filter(!isnan, w.speed[good]))     # the true speed is 0.5
0.498409265756032
2026-10-15 — Speeds relative to the agents

Decided: ω is each agent's own phase advance, so speeds are relative to the agents. Why: the question is how a signal travels through the tissue, not how far the tissue itself moves. The prototype also computed a speed with one global period for all cells; per-agent ω is the honest version and is the one kept.

The neighbourhood radius

Tierbrief

Waves shorter than about 2r cannot be read: neighbours more than half a wavelength away wrap past ±π and the fit fails.

Tierfull

The plane fit works only while the true phase differences inside the neighbourhood stay within ±π, that is, while the wavelength exceeds about twice the radius. Slow waves have short wavelengths (λ = c·T), so they need a small radius; a small radius has fewer neighbours and noisier fits. Run two radii: if direction and clean share agree, the radius is not the limit. The share of clean fits collapses where the limit is crossed, so it warns of the problem.

Synthetic plane waves (period 180 minutes, cortex-like density, slowly moving agents) through the whole pipeline, as in the test suite:
